# P2-09: Money sinks (taxes, imports, community fund, two-stage spoilage)

**Status:** Completed

**Role:** Economy
**Branch:** task/P2-09-money-out
**Depends on:** P2-08

## Goal
Money leaves the village through its named sinks — taxes, imports, the
community fund, the feast reserve, and spoilage — and the stabilizers
(prosperity-scaled tax, adaptive import ordering) keep the village total in
its ±10% band. The economy becomes a closed loop with gates.

## Context
ECONOMY.md §6. The worked warm month: inflow 1,550 (merchants 800 +
travelers 750) vs. outflow 1,500 (taxes 400 + imports 600 + community 150 +
spoilage 150 + feast reserve 200). "The simulation must reproduce this:
total village copper stays within a ±10% band month to month, with the
seasonal swing carried by storage (grain, cellared apples, the fund)."

Sink rules (all from the design doc):
1. **Taxes** — the reeve collects twice a year (spring and autumn). The 20
   pay ~1,200 per collection: Corvin 180, Tilda 200, Bessa 150, Mira 120,
   Oda 100, Doran 100, Garrick 80, the rest smaller (scale the remainder
   proportionally; document the table). **The assessment scales with visible
   prosperity** — the stabilizer: good years pay more. Paid to the crown:
   money leaves the village.
2. **Imports** — P2-08 built the merchant sell side; here add the adaptive
   rule: **Tilda orders more imports when the village has coin and fewer
   when it doesn't** (document the prosperity signal and the scaling).
   ~600/month in warm months is the calibration target, not a quota.
3. **Community fund** — the council sets aside ~150/month (Garrick's
   8,000-copper wheel is the long goal). WorldState-owned fund state with
   internal capture/restore (P2-12 pattern). This is savings, not destruction
   — it stays in the village total but out of circulation.
4. **Feast reserve** — 200 copper set aside for the Harvest Feast (autumn).
5. **Two-stage spoilage** — the `perishable` block in `Content/items/items.json`
   is the spec: `freshDays` fresh → `staleDays` stale (stale name, effect,
   `stalePriceFactor` 0.5) → spoiled (removed to compost/feed, value
   destroyed). Track per-lot age in inventories; stale goods sell/eat at the
   reduced value. Calibration: ~150/month of value destroyed in a full
   village (the acceptance sim will check the band, not the exact number).

## Requirements
1. Tax collection: spring + autumn visits, per-NPC assessments from the
   documented table, prosperity-scaled (document the scaling rule — e.g. a
   visible-prosperity index from total village copper vs. baseline). Money
   leaves to the crown; events logged.
2. Adaptive import ordering extends P2-08's merchant flow (or its order
   list): order size scales with the prosperity signal; prove in a test
   that a rich village imports more than a poor one.
3. Community fund + feast reserve: monthly set-aside with the documented
   amounts; fund state persists via the internal convention.
4. Spoilage: per-lot aging in `Inventory` (or a companion state — your
   choice, document it); fresh → stale → spoiled transitions emit quiet
   events; stale goods trade at the price factor and apply the stale
   effect when eaten; spoiled lots are removed (compost).
5. Money conservation audit: taxes/imports destroy village copper only
   through the named gates; the community fund/feast reserve move copper
   into named pots (still counted in the village total).

## Tests required
- Spoilage: a 3-day pork lot goes fresh → stale (price halves, effect
  changes) → spoiled (removed); non-perishables never spoil.
- Tax: spring collection takes ~1,200 total with the documented per-NPC
  split; a prosperous village pays more than a poor one.
- Adaptive imports: rich village orders more than poor village.
- Fund + feast reserve accumulate on schedule; a month-long run keeps
  total village copper within the ±10% band (sources from P2-08 minus
  these sinks).
- Save → load keeps fund, tax state, spoilage ages (internal convention).
